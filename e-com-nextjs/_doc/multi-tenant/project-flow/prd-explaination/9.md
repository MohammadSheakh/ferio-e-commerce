# Lesson 9 — Plans, Subscriptions, Entitlements & SaaS Billing 💰

Now we answer a very important question:

> **How does Ferio decide what each business is allowed to use—and how does Ferio get paid?**

Imagine ABC Electronics signs up for Ferio.

```text
ABC Electronics
      ↓
14-day trial
      ↓
Business Plan
      ↓
Pays Ferio monthly
      ↓
Gets certain features + limits
```

This whole system is **MT-6** in your project. Your checklist says its goal is to enforce subscription state and plan limits **server-side**, not merely hide buttons in the frontend.

They're related, but they are NOT the same thing.

```text

```

Let's make this concrete.

Suppose Ferio offers:

Your project has resolved those as the plan structure, although final pricing is still described as pilot-dependent in the checklist.

# 4. Entitlement = "Can you use this?"

This is one of the most important words in SaaS.

Suppose Business Plan allows:

```text

```

Each of these rules is an **entitlement**.

Your project's `PlanEntitlement` essentially stores:

```text
featureKey

```



So conceptually:

```text
Plan: BUSINESS

─────────────────────────
```

# 5. Feature entitlement vs limit entitlement

There are basically two easy mental categories.

### Yes/No feature

```text
advanced_reports = TRUE
```

Question:

> Can ABC use advanced reports?

Answer:

```text
YES
```

### Numeric limit

```text
products_max = 1000
```

Question:

> How many products may ABC have?

Answer:

```text
1000
```

So:

```text
ENTITLEMENT

      ┌──────────────┐
      │              │
      ▼              ▼
 Feature flag       Limit

 yes / no          maximum
```

---

# 6. Why not simply write this in frontend?

Suppose Starter doesn't allow Advanced Reports.

Frontend hides:

```text
[ Advanced Reports ]
```

Great?

**No.**

A clever user could directly call:

```http
GET /api/v1/reports/advanced
```

If backend doesn't check the entitlement:

```text
Frontend:
hidden ❌

Backend:
allowed ✅
```

then your plan restriction is fake.

Therefore:

> **Entitlements must be enforced server-side.**

Your implementation does exactly that and returns stable denial codes such as `FEATURE_DISABLED`, `PLAN_LIMIT_REACHED` and `SUBSCRIPTION_INACTIVE`.

# 10. UsageCounter

The Control Plane can keep something conceptually like:

```text
UsageCounter
────────────────────
organization = ABC
metric       = orders_per_month
period       = 2026-09
value        = 2734
```

Notice:

```text
period = 2026-09
```

Why?

Because:

```text
orders this month
```

resets for the next billing/monthly period.

Your implementation scopes applicable counters by a `YYYY-MM` UTC period key. 

So:

```text
September
orders = 4,921

October starts

orders = 0
```

Historical data isn't deleted. The **usage counter for the new period** simply starts separately.

---

# 11. But product count works differently

Imagine:

```text
products_max = 1000
```

You don't want October 1st to magically say:

```text
Products used = 0
```

when ABC actually has 742 products.

😂

That's why different metrics can be calculated differently.

Your registry distinguishes real-time monetizable counters from metrics that can be recounted from actual facts. 

Think:

```text
orders_per_month
      ↓
increment as orders happen


products_max
      ↓
can recount actual products


staff_seats
      ↓
can recount actual memberships
```

---

# 12. What if UsageCounter becomes wrong?

Suppose database truth says:

```text
Products = 742
```

but some bug causes Control Plane counter to say:

```text
Products = 739
```

You need:

# Reconciliation

Remember this word from commerce?

Same idea:

> Compare the summary/counter against the authoritative facts and correct drift.

```text
UsageCounter says:
739

Tenant DB says:
742

      ↓

DRIFT DETECTED

      ↓

correct counter
```

Your `UsageReconciliationService` recounts order/catalog facts from tenant DBs and seats from control-plane memberships, and records drift warnings when needed.

# 16. PAST_DUE

Suppose ABC's subscription payment becomes overdue.

Should Ferio immediately shut down ABC's entire website?

Your chosen architecture says no.

There's a:

```text
7-day grace period
```

and during `PAST_DUE`, the storefront remains browsable. 

Conceptually:

```text
Payment overdue
      ↓
PAST_DUE
      ↓
Grace period
```

This gives the tenant time to fix billing.

---

# 17. SUSPENDED

If the subscription reaches the suspension state, your policy is deliberately not:

```text
DELETE EVERYTHING 💥
```

Instead, your checklist says the storefront stays browsable while checkout is denied. 

Think:

```text
ABC subscription
SUSPENDED

Customer:
Can view products     ✅
Can browse store      ✅
Can checkout          ❌
```

That's much safer than destroying tenant data.

---

```text
    ↓
```

# 20. Upgrade

Suppose Baby Shop has Starter.

```text
Product limit = 100
```

They now have:

```text
100 products
```

They attempt product #101.

Backend:

```text
PLAN_LIMIT_REACHED
```

Admin dashboard can say:

```text
You've reached your plan limit.

[ Upgrade ]
```

They upgrade:

```text
Starter
   ↓
Business
```

Now entitlement evaluator sees a larger limit.

```text
Create product #101
       ↓
ALLOW ✅
```

Your actual integration test proves this lifecycle against real PostgreSQL: a limit is hit, the operation is denied without partial state, upgrade unlocks it, and downgrade enforces the lower limit again. 

That's a very important test.

# 21. Upgrade should NOT require moving their data

This is another nice property of the design.

Bad:

```text

    ↓
upgrade
    ↓
copy everything
    ↓
Business DB
```

Unnecessary.

Instead:

```text
ABC DB
stays ABC DB

Subscription:
Starter → Business

Entitlements:
change
```

Your entitlement tests specifically verify that an upgrade can unlock capability without a tenant DB migration where possible.

# 22. Downgrade is more interesting

Suppose Business allows more usage.

ABC has historical orders.

Then ABC downgrades.

Should Ferio say:

> "You're on a cheaper plan. Delete your old orders."

Absolutely not.

Your test explicitly verifies that historical orders survive upgrade and downgrade unchanged. 

Instead:

```text
Existing historical data
       ↓
KEEP IT

Future restricted operation
       ↓
possibly DENY
```

That's an important distinction:

> **Limit future capability rather than destructively deleting historical business data.**

# 25. Why mixing them would be terrible

Imagine Ferio's SaaS subscription payment gets inserted into ABC's normal:

```text

Wallet
Settlement
```

Then ABC's commerce report might say:

```text

৳500,000
```

but part of it is actually:

```text

```

😵

Financial reporting becomes nonsense.

Your architecture explicitly prevents `PlatformBillingService` from writing SaaS payments into tenant `Payment`, `Wallet`, COD, refund or settlement records. 

This boundary is even architecture-tested.

# 30. PlanVersion — why does this exist?

This is a more advanced but important idea.

Suppose in January:

```text
Business Plan
products_max = 1000
```

In June Ferio changes it to:

```text
products_max = 2000
```

Later someone asks:

> "What exactly did the Business Plan mean when this decision/payment happened?"

If you constantly overwrite everything without history, it becomes difficult to explain.

Your architecture versions plan changes and includes normalized entitlement snapshots in auditing, while keeping billing history in the Control Plane. 

Think:

```text
Business Plan v1
      ↓
Business Plan v2
      ↓
Business Plan v3
```

Now changes can be explained historically.

# 31. Warning thresholds

Suppose ABC gets:

```text
5,000 orders/month
```

You shouldn't wait until:

```text
5,001
```

to surprise them.

Ferio's usage registry supports warning thresholds and emits a warning when a configured threshold boundary is crossed. 

Conceptually:

```text
2,500 / 5,000
50%

4,000 / 5,000
80%
      ↓
⚠ usage warning

4,500 / 5,000
90%
      ↓
⚠ another threshold

5,000 / 5,000
100%
```

The exact thresholds are configuration/registry details; the idea is to warn before hard denial where appropriate.

# 36. One architecture detail you should really understand

Notice where the decision happens:

```text
Frontend ❌

```

Frontend can display:

```text

```

but the actual enforcement must happen:

